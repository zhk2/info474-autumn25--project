Understanding Tariffs

A tariff is a tax imposed by one country on goods and services imported from another country. Tariffs serve multiple strategic purposes: protecting domestic industries from foreign competition, generating government revenue, and addressing trade imbalances.

The US trade policy from 2018-2024 marked a significant shift toward protectionism, with tariffs imposed on hundreds of billions of dollars worth of goods.

Consumer Impact

Each line shows a major import category for the United States: electronics, chemicals, manufactured goods, and mineral fuels. Values are normalized between 0 and 1, so the height of each line reflects how reliant the US is on imports for that category rather than exact dollar amounts.

The shaded vertical bands mark years with notable tariff hikes. Electronics start out as the most import dependent category and dip around the height of the trade war before climbing again. Chemicals rise more sharply after 2020, while manufactures and mineral fuels stay relatively low and stable. Together, these patterns suggest that tariff policy does not hit all goods equally and that some consumer facing sectors remain highly exposed to imported inputs even after tensions ease.

The Future of Trade

This view compares three periods in the recent trade story: the pre trade war years (2015–2017), the peak of tariff tensions (2018–2020), and the more recent period (2021–2024).

Each point plots tariff tension on the horizontal axis (the share of tariff changes that were increases) against trade volatility on the vertical axis (how much total global trade swung year to year). The 2018–2020 period stands out with both higher tariff tension and larger swings in trade, while 2015–2017 and 2021–2024 cluster closer together with lower volatility.

This pattern suggests that intense tariff battles can coincide with more unstable trade, but that volatility can fall back even when tariffs remain above pre trade war levels. In other words, policy shocks leave a mark, yet the long term path of global trade is still uncertain.
